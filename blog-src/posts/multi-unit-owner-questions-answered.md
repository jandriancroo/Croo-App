I ran four restaurant locations over 12 years, and the questions I get from other owners sound a lot like the ones I used to ask myself. Here are seven of them, with the answers I'd give over coffee. These are my opinions as an operator, not research findings.

## 1. When do I need a second manager level, like an area manager?

When you notice you're the only thing connecting your stores. If a problem in store two waits for you to drive over, or two GMs handle the same issue two different ways, you've got a gap. A shared routine often fixes it before a new title does. Start with the same [opening and closing checklists](https://croohq.com/blog/opening-closing-checklists-fast-casual/) in every store so your managers are working from the same page.

## 2. How do I know what's happening in a store I'm not in?

You need three things you can check without calling anyone: the schedule against the clock, whether the shift's tasks got done, and the day's sales against labor. If you can see those from your phone, you can skip most of the "how's it going?" calls. The longer version is in [running three stores without living in your car](https://croohq.com/blog/running-three-stores-without-being-everywhere/).

## 3. Why does one store always run over on labor?

Usually it's a habit, not a person. It might be a late-night overlap that never got trimmed, or swaps nobody checked, or punches edited without a reason. Look at the schedule against the punches for two weeks before blaming the GM. Rules like [swap approval](https://croohq.com/blog/shift-swap-rules-restaurants/) tend to fix more than a talking-to.

## 4. Can a manager handle a call-out without calling me?

They should, and the owner's job is to make that easy. A manager needs to see who's available, who's close to overtime, and who wants hours. That's a lot to hold in your head at 5 PM. This is one of the places Theo helps. He's the voice AI agent inside CrooHQ, so a manager can ask him to help find coverage, with labor in mind, while they stay on the floor. He doesn't replace the manager's call on who to trust, but he cuts the phone list out.

## 5. What should I standardize across stores, and what should I leave alone?

Standardize the things that protect money and safety: how people punch, how swaps and edits are approved, what a close looks like, and how a shift gets handed off. Leave the local stuff alone: the music, the regulars, how a team likes to run its line. A good rule is that anything you'd want to audit should be the same everywhere. The [shift handoff note](https://croohq.com/blog/shift-handoff-notes-restaurant-managers/) is a great place to begin because it costs two minutes and pays back every morning.

## 6. How do I keep payroll out of the middle of all this?

Keep payroll doing payroll. CrooHQ feeds payroll and QuickBooks; it doesn't replace them, and I wouldn't want it to. The point is to hand your payroll provider clean punches and a schedule that matches, so the Monday questions get shorter. The federal [recordkeeping rules](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/21-flsa-recordkeeping) ask for accurate hours worked each day and week, and that goes a lot more smoothly when the records come from one honest source.

## 7. What's the first thing I should fix?

Pick the thing that costs you a phone call every week. For most owners it's one of three: the schedule living in a group chat, punches that don't match, or a closer who leaves without a handoff. Fix one, hold it for a month, then fix the next. Trying to change everything in all stores at once is how crews stop trusting the change.

If it's the schedule, start with [getting it out of the group text](https://croohq.com/blog/schedule-group-text/). If it's punches, the [missed punch policy template](https://croohq.com/blog/missed-punch-policy-template-restaurants/) is a good first page.

## What crews and managers get out of it

Owners sometimes ask me whether crews like this. The honest answer is that they like it when it's fair and when it saves them a headache: a schedule they can trust, a clock that matches what they worked, a manager who can find coverage without a 20-text chain. When those things work, the crew doesn't think about software at all. That's the goal.
