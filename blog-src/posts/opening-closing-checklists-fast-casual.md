Every fast-casual store I've walked has an opening checklist. Most also have a closing checklist. The ones that struggle aren't missing paper. They're missing a list the crew finishes when the line is slammed and the manager is on a call.

I ran Jersey Mike's locations and I run Blaze Pizza. The days the open went smooth weren't the days we had the longest laminated sheet. They were the days the list lived where the crew already was, and the closer knew the opener would see what got skipped.

## Why paper and "the binder" fail on a real shift

A checklist fails for boring reasons:

- **It's in the office.** The work is on the line, in the walk-in, at the front counter.
- **Nobody owns the last three items.** "Deep clean the soda nozzles" becomes optional when the last guest won't leave.
- **The opener never sees the closer's night.** So the same miss repeats for a week.
- **Multi-unit owners find out from a guest review.** Not from a store that flagged it.

Crew don't hate checklists. They hate fake ones — the ones that exist for the auditor, not for the shift. The FDA's [Food Code](https://www.fda.gov/food/retail-food-protection/fda-food-code) is the public playbook a lot of local health rules lean on; your brand standards sit on top of that. Software doesn't replace either. It just stops the "we initialed it" theater.

## What belongs on an opening list (fast-casual)

Keep it short enough to finish before the door unlocks. Long lists teach people to initial without looking.

**FOH open**

1. Floors, tables, and lobby walk — guest-ready, not "we mopped yesterday."
2. Condiment / napkin / lid stations stocked for the first rush.
3. POS and online-order tablet awake, printers tested, cash drawer counted if you use one.
4. Music, lights, and lobby temperature set the way your brand standard says.
5. Restroom check before the first guest, not after the first complaint.

**BOH open**

1. Walk-in and reach-in temps logged.
2. Prep levels vs. the day's forecast — not vs. "what we usually do."
3. Protein / produce FIFO confirmed on the rail.
4. Dish pit empty enough to start, not already buried from last night.
5. Equipment on and safe: ovens, make line, dish machine.

If an item doesn't change the guest experience or food safety in the first two hours, it can live on a mid-shift or weekly deep-clean list instead.

## What belongs on a closing list

Closing is where good stores protect tomorrow's open.

**FOH close**

1. Dining room reset so the opener isn't doing last night's work.
2. Trash and recycling out — lobby smell is a brand problem.
3. Deposit / drawer done to your cash policy.
4. Doors, lights, and alarm the same way every night.
5. A two-line note for the opener: what broke, what ran out, who to call.

**BOH close**

1. Make line broken down and cleaned to your standard, not "wiped."
2. Walk-in organized; nothing left on the floor "for morning."
3. Dish pit clear; soak pans labeled if they need time.
4. Temps logged again if your procedure requires it.
5. Tomorrow's thaw / prep pulled per the prep list.

The handoff note is the underrated item. A checklist without a handoff is how openers inherit mysteries — which is why [shift handoff notes](https://croohq.com/blog/shift-handoff-notes-restaurant-managers/) deserve their own habit, not a blank line at the bottom of a PDF.
