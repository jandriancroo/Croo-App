The best closers I ever had weren't just clean. They were specific.

Not "long night, good luck." More like: "Walk-in door sticking — don't force it. Short on ranch, truck Thursday. Devon covered Maria's last two hours; she's at 40. Online tablet crashed once around 8:40, reboot fixed it."

Two minutes to write. An hour saved for the opener. That gap is where multi-unit operators either look organized or look like they live in their car.

## Why handoffs die in restaurants

Handoffs fail for the same reasons [paper checklists](https://croohq.com/blog/opening-closing-checklists-fast-casual/) fail:

- They're optional culture, not a required step before clock-out.
- They live in a notebook nobody opens, or a group chat that scrolls away.
- Closers write novels nobody reads — or one vague sentence that helps no one.
- Openers stop trusting the note, so they stop reading it, so closers stop writing it.

When the handoff dies, the opener rebuilds reality from cold prep levels, a messy dish pit, and whoever happens to show up early. That's not leadership. That's archaeology. The National Restaurant Association's [education and operations resources](https://restaurant.org/education-and-resources/) keep circling the same theme operators already feel: consistency across shifts is the product.

## The two-minute handoff template

Require this format. Short beats thorough that never happens.

1. **People.** Call-outs, covers, early sends, anyone close to overtime, anyone who needs a conversation tomorrow.
2. **Product.** What you 86'd, what you're short on, what must be prepped first.
3. **Equipment.** Anything broken, intermittent, or jury-rigged — and whether a ticket was already put in.
4. **Guest / brand.** Complaints that need a follow-up, a VIP note, a delivery issue that will rebound at lunch.
5. **Money / admin.** Drawer variance, deposits, anything the opening manager must touch before the rush.
6. **Tomorrow's trap.** The one thing that will bite the next shift if they walk in cold.

If it doesn't fit those six bullets, it probably belongs in a longer manager log or a weekly GM note — not the nightly handoff.

## Tie the handoff to the real schedule and punches

A handoff that says "Devon closed for Maria" while the schedule still shows Maria is how you create [Monday punch cleanup](https://croohq.com/blog/punches-dont-match/). Update the schedule when the cover happens. If the cover came from a late quit text, treat coverage and the note as one motion — same muscle as [covering after a 10:02 PM resignation](https://croohq.com/blog/closer-quit-text-theo-coverage/).

Swaps that only exist in a text thread are how openers get the wrong person and the wrong hours. Keep the truth in the schedule, not in a muted chat, the same way you'd [get the schedule out of the group text](https://croohq.com/blog/schedule-group-text/).

Personal PINs and eyes on the clock still matter too. A handoff won't catch [buddy punching](https://croohq.com/blog/buddy-punching/), but a clean close plus a clock that knows the schedule makes "who was actually here" a lot less mysterious.
