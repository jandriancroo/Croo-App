Most shift swaps are a good sign. Two people sorted out their own week, nobody called you at 9 PM, and the shift is covered. That's your crew taking ownership.

The trouble is the swap that nobody ran past the schedule. Say Maria trades her Saturday close to Devon. Devon already has 36 hours. Now he's on the clock for 44, you find out Monday, and a favor between two friends turned into overtime you never planned.

Swaps aren't the problem. Unwritten swap rules are.

## Why swaps quietly cost you

A swap changes three things at once: who is on the floor, how many hours each person has, and who your manager thinks is coming in. If only the first one gets handled, the other two drift.

- **Hours drift.** The person picking up the shift crosses into overtime without anyone deciding that was okay.
- **Skill drift.** A trained closer swaps with someone who has never closed. The schedule says "closer" and the store gets a first-timer.
- **Paper drift.** The swap lives in a text thread, the schedule still shows the old name, and the punches on Monday don't match, the exact [Monday punch cleanup](https://croohq.com/blog/punches-dont-match/) most managers dread.

Federal overtime rules are built around the workweek, so a swap that moves hours between people can change who owes overtime. The U.S. Department of Labor's [recordkeeping fact sheet](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/21-flsa-recordkeeping) lists the hours worked each day and each workweek among the records an employer has to keep accurately. A swap that never makes it into the schedule makes that harder than it needs to be.

## The six swap rules I'd write down

Keep them short enough to fit on one page in the break area.

1. **Swaps are approved by a manager, always.** Crew members propose, a manager says yes. It takes ten seconds and it's the whole safety net.
2. **Check hours before you say yes.** The manager looks at the picking-up person's week. If the swap pushes them near overtime, it's a no or a conversation, not a shrug.
3. **Same role, same station, or trained.** A swap is like for like. A closer swaps with a closer. If someone is learning a station, a lead signs off first.
4. **Swap deadline.** Pick a cutoff, such as 24 hours before the shift, for routine swaps. Anything later is a call-out and handled as one.
5. **The swap person owns the shift.** If the shift is a no-show, the person who agreed to take it carries it, not the original person and not the manager.
6. **The schedule changes the same day.** The approved swap goes into the schedule right away. If it isn't in the schedule, it didn't happen.

Rule six is the one managers skip. It is also the one that keeps the clock, the schedule, and payroll telling the same story.

## What a clean swap looks like

Here's a simple hypothetical to show the flow, not a real story.

Maria can't work Saturday close. She asks Devon in the crew chat, and he says yes. Instead of stopping there, Maria sends the request to the manager. The manager opens the week, sees Devon at 28 hours, sees that he has closed before, and approves. The schedule updates before anyone goes home. On Saturday, Devon's punch matches his scheduled shift, and Monday has nothing to chase.

Now change one detail: Devon is at 38 hours. Same swap, different answer. The manager offers the shift to someone at 22 hours who can also close. Maria still gets her Saturday, and nobody earns overtime by accident.

## Where this goes wrong in a group text

The swap conversation usually happens in a group chat, and a group chat is a bad system of record. Messages scroll. Two people say "I got it." Someone never reads it. If you're still running the schedule that way, [getting the schedule out of the group text](https://croohq.com/blog/schedule-group-text/) fixes more swap trouble than any rule will.

## The crew side of it

Crews like swap rules, which surprises some owners. A clear rule means nobody gets burned for trusting a coworker, nobody gets blamed for a no-show that wasn't theirs, and nobody has to wonder whether a favor is "allowed." Fair beats flexible when flexible depends on who the manager likes.

For a multi-unit owner it matters more. You can't referee swaps in three stores.
