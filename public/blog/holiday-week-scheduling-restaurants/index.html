Holiday Week Scheduling: Thanksgiving Wednesday and Black Friday at a Fast-Casual Store

Jordan Andrian, Founder, CrooHQ
November 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Thanksgiving week doesn't behave like a normal week, and the schedule you built for a normal week won't survive it. Wednesday can be strange in either direction. Black Friday can be a surprise. And your crew has families, travel, and a long weekend on their minds.

I ran four locations for twelve years, and the holiday weeks I handled best all had one thing in common: the hard decisions got made early, in writing, while there was still time to fix them. Here's the plan I'd use at a fast-casual store.

Start with the three days that matter

Don't try to perfect the whole week. Look at three days:

  • Wednesday before Thanksgiving. Pull what your own store did on this day last year and the year before. Some locations get a late-afternoon spike, others go quiet. Your history beats any rule of thumb, including mine.
  • Thanksgiving Day. Decide now whether you're open, and if so, what hours. Tell the crew the same day you decide.
  • Black Friday. Same process. Look at your own past Fridays after Thanksgiving, not at a generic "busy day" assumption.

If you only have one year of history, say so and plan a manager's eyes on the floor to adjust mid-shift. Don't pretend you know more than you do.

Collect time-off requests first, then build

The mistake I see most is building the schedule and then handling time-off requests one at a time. Flip it:

  1. Set a deadline for holiday availability, ideally two to three weeks out. Put the deadline in front of the crew in writing.
  2. Collect every request in one place.
  3. Mark what's truly non-negotiable (travel already booked) and what's a preference.
  4. Build the schedule around the non-negotiables, then fill in.

Then be fair about the unpopular shifts. If the same two people close every holiday, you'll hear about it, and you should. Rotate who gets the hard shifts year to year and tell your crew that's how you do it.

Build the call-out plan before anyone calls out

Holiday weeks produce call-outs. Plan for them the way you'd plan for a slow dishwasher: assume it happens.

  • Name a backup for each key position on Wednesday and Friday: opener, closer, line, register.
  • Write down who has said yes to extra hours, and who is near 40 hours already. The reliable person you'd text first may be the one you can't afford to add. The same overtime rule behind shift swap rules applies here.
  • Decide who's on call at the manager level. One person, with a phone, on each of the busy days.

Handle the swaps before they handle you

Holiday weeks are swap season. Everyone's trading, and everything gets agreed in a group text. Before the week starts, remind the crew of your swap rule: swaps go through the schedule, a manager approves them, and no one gets pushed into overtime by a favor. A written rule takes thirty seconds to repeat and saves you a Monday full of surprises.

A manager checklist for the week

Print or share this with every manager on a holiday week:

  • Final schedule posted by the date you promised.
  • Holiday hours confirmed and announced to the crew, and to guests where it applies.
  • Backup named for each key position on Wednesday and Friday.
  • Opening and closing checklists reviewed, and anyone new to a holiday shift paired with someone experienced. Our opening and closing checklist is a good start.
  • Ordering and deliveries checked against the holiday delivery calendar. Many vendors change schedules around the holiday, so confirm directly with yours.
  • Shift handoff notes in place so Thursday's and Friday's managers aren't guessing. Here is how to write a handoff note managers will read.

Thank people in a way they notice

Crews remember holiday weeks. A short, specific thank-you from you, in person if you can, does more than a general message. Tell people what you saw. "You covered Wednesday close on short notice and the store still opened clean Friday" lands better than "thanks, team."

The short version

Decide early, write it down, and have a plan for the call-out you hope doesn't come. A holiday week doesn't have to be a scramble if the work happens a few weeks before it.
