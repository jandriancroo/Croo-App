The Mock Health Inspection: A 20-Minute Walk-Through Checklist for Multi-Unit Operators

Jordan Andrian, Founder, CrooHQ
November 25, 2026 · 4 min read

The best time to find a problem is before the inspector does. A mock inspection is just that: you walk your own store the way an inspector would, write down what you find, and fix it. Twenty minutes, once a week or once a month, can change how a real visit feels.

Here's the walk-through I'd use. It's a checklist for the routine things that tend to slip in a busy store. It is not a legal standard.

First, know whose rules apply

Your local health department sets the rules you're inspected against. Many are based on the FDA Food Code, which the FDA describes as a model for food control jurisdictions at all levels of government. Each state and county adopts its own version, so the details differ by location. Before you build your checklist, get your local inspection form from your health department and use it as your template. If you run stores in more than one county or state, expect a different form for each.

I'm giving you a general structure. Your health department's form is the authority.

The walk-through, in the order an inspector might go

Start at the front door and move toward the back. Take your phone or clipboard and write down what you see, not what you hope is true.

### 1. People and hygiene (3 minutes) - Handwashing sinks are accessible, stocked with soap and paper towels, and not used for anything else. - Crew members are following your hair, glove, and jewelry rules. - Anyone who is sick is not working food. Ask your managers to tell you how they handle this. - Manager on duty can explain your illness-reporting policy.

### 2. Temperatures (4 minutes) - Walk-in, reach-ins, and prep coolers have working thermometers, and you've checked them against the temperature log. - Hot-held items and cold-held items are checked with a probe, and the probe itself is clean and calibrated. - Anything that's out of range is logged with what you did about it. - The temperature log is filled in for today, not backfilled.

### 3. Storage (4 minutes) - Everything is labeled and dated. - Raw proteins are stored below ready-to-eat foods. - Nothing is stored on the floor. - First in, first out is visible: older product in front. - Chemicals are stored away from food and prep surfaces.

### 4. Cleaning and sanitizing (4 minutes) - Sanitizer buckets or dispensers are at the right strength. Check with test strips. - Food-contact surfaces look and feel clean, including slicers, prep boards, and the inside of ice machines where visible. - Wiping cloths are in sanitizer between uses. - The three-compartment sink or dish machine is set up correctly.

### 5. Facility and pests (3 minutes) - Floors, walls, and ceiling tiles are in good repair and clean. - Back door closes fully, with no gaps. - No signs of pests: droppings, gnaw marks, or live insects. - Trash is covered and taken out on schedule.

### 6. Paperwork (2 minutes) - Food handler cards or required certifications are current for the crew, and you can find them in a minute. - Your last inspection report is on hand. - Temperature, cleaning, and any other logs are where the manager can pull them quickly.

Requirements for certifications and records vary by jurisdiction, so check what yours asks for.

Grade yourself honestly

For each item, mark one of three: good, needs attention, or fix now. Fix-now items are things you'd correct while the inspector watches, such as a missing soap dispenser. Needs-attention items go on a list with an owner and a date.

Don't average it out. A store with one serious temperature problem isn't an "80 percent store." It's a store with a temperature problem.

Close the loop, or the walk-through is wasted

The checklist only helps if the findings turn into action:

  1. Assign each issue to a person with a due date.
  2. Re-check the fix. Don't take "done" as proof.
  3. Keep the finished walk-through so you can see trends over several visits.
  4. Share the pattern with the other stores. If one location keeps missing the same thing, the others probably do too.

If you're running several stores, rotate who does the walk-through. A manager from another location often sees what the home team has stopped noticing, which is part of running three stores without being everywhere.
