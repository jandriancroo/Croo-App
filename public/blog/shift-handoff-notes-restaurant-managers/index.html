Shift Handoff Notes: What the Closing Manager Must Pass On

Jordan Andrian, Founder, CrooHQ
November 9, 2026 · 3 min read

The best closers I ever had weren't just clean. They were specific.

Not "long night, good luck." More like: "Walk-in door sticking — don't force it. Short on ranch, truck Thursday. Devon covered Maria's last two hours; she's at 40. Online tablet crashed once around 8:40, reboot fixed it."

Two minutes to write. An hour saved for the opener. That gap is where multi-unit operators either look organized or look like they live in their car.

Why handoffs die in restaurants

Handoffs fail for the same reasons paper checklists fail:

  • They're optional culture, not a required step before clock-out.
  • They live in a notebook nobody opens, or a group chat that scrolls away.
  • Closers write novels nobody reads — or one vague sentence that helps no one.
  • Openers stop trusting the note, so they stop reading it, so closers stop writing it.

When the handoff dies, the opener rebuilds reality from cold prep levels, a messy dish pit, and whoever happens to show up early. That's not leadership. That's archaeology. The National Restaurant Association's education and operations resources keep circling the same theme operators already feel: consistency across shifts is the product.

The two-minute handoff template

Require this format. Short beats thorough that never happens.

  1. People. Call-outs, covers, early sends, anyone close to overtime, anyone who needs a conversation tomorrow.
  2. Product. What you 86'd, what you're short on, what must be prepped first.
  3. Equipment. Anything broken, intermittent, or jury-rigged — and whether a ticket was already put in.
  4. Guest / brand. Complaints that need a follow-up, a VIP note, a delivery issue that will rebound at lunch.
  5. Money / admin. Drawer variance, deposits, anything the opening manager must touch before the rush.
  6. Tomorrow's trap. The one thing that will bite the next shift if they walk in cold.

If it doesn't fit those six bullets, it probably belongs in a longer manager log or a weekly GM note — not the nightly handoff.

Tie the handoff to the real schedule and punches

A handoff that says "Devon closed for Maria" while the schedule still shows Maria is how you create Monday punch cleanup. Update the schedule when the cover happens. If the cover came from a late quit text, treat coverage and the note as one motion — same muscle as covering after a 10:02 PM resignation.

Swaps that only exist in a text thread are how openers get the wrong person and the wrong hours. Keep the truth in the schedule, not in a muted chat, the same way you'd get the schedule out of the group text.

Personal PINs and eyes on the clock still matter too. A handoff won't catch buddy punching, but a clean close plus a clock that knows the schedule makes "who was actually here" a lot less mysterious.

Make it a habit managers don't skip

People finish what the system asks for at the natural end of the work. If the handoff is a separate binder in the office, it loses to getting out the door. If it's part of the same close flow as the checklist — in CrooHQ, next to the tasks the closer already completes — it survives busy nights.

Multi-unit owners feel this immediately. A store that hands off well needs fewer morning calls. A store that doesn't will keep pulling you into the parking lot, which is the whole problem in running three locations without being everywhere.
